Structural Drivers & Wholesale Market Fundamentals

Why free electricity exists: grid fundamentals

The commercial viability of free domestic electricity depends directly on the physical congestion and market clearing architecture of the British electricity network, coordinated by NESO and settled by Elexon.

Renewable Curtailment

Generation from offshore and onshore wind often exceeds what transmission lines can carry south — especially across the Anglo-Scottish B6 boundary. Rather than pay generators to curtail (funded by BSUoS charges, £1bn+ in 2023), NESO and DNOs now pay suppliers to increase consumption instead.

Negative Clearing

When forecast wind/solar surges alongside low demand, EPEX SPOT day-ahead auctions can clear below zero — subsidised generators and inflexible nuclear baseload would rather pay offtakers than shut down. Buyers get credited cash per MWh they absorb.

Imbalance Cash-Out

Elexon reconciles contracted vs. actual consumption every 30-minute Settlement Period. A supplier long on energy during a negative-price period must pay cash for the surplus — triggering a free session shifts that surplus onto customers instead.

Predictive Modelling, Systems Architecture & Algorithmic Dispatch

How suppliers predict a free-electricity window

Executing this at retail scale takes an integrated software stack: forecasting renewable output down to network nodes, forecasting demand, and orchestrating distributed devices in real time — on a timeline set by the British power market.

D − Day-ahead, 09:00–12:00
Day-Ahead Market Clearing

EPEX SPOT day-ahead auction results clear between 09:20 and 11:00. If clearing prices fall near or below zero, retail platforms schedule a Free Electricity Session. Push notifications and emails go out between 10:00–14:00, detailing timing and baseline terms.

4–8 hours ahead
Intraday & Local Network Scheduling

DNOs and NESO finalize short-term network constraint forecasts. For localized schemes like Octopus Power-ups, regional tenders are confirmed around 10:00 on the day of delivery for early-afternoon execution.

T − 60 minutes
Real-Time Balancing Gate Closure

One hour before each half-hour settlement period, Gate Closure occurs — suppliers submit Final Physical Notifications to Elexon and NESO. DERMS engines then dispatch telemetry signals to EV chargers and home inverters, ramping loads (e.g. 7.4 kW EV chargers, 3.0 kW home batteries) to absorb the surplus.

Financial Settlement Formulas, P&L Balance & Budget Allocation

The formulas behind the free electricity credit

Free electricity mechanisms operate under defined accounting rules that reconcile customer baselines, wholesale settlement, network charges, and balancing cash flows.

Customer baseline & bill credit
  • baseline consumption for customer i, settlement period t
  • set of reference historical baseline days (10 preceding weekdays, or 4 preceding weekend days)
  • customer's standard contracted retail unit rate (p/kWh)

The customer pays their normal rate up to the baseline and receives all incremental volume free; standing charges are unaffected.

Net supplier margin per session
  • wholesale/flexibility revenue (negative price offtake + LCM/DNO constraint payments)
  • network & policy levies (DUoS, BSUoS, environmental levies) for that period

When the wholesale price is negative, the supplier is paid to offtake power — turning cost into revenue. LCM (Local Constraint Market) payments from NESO/DNOs typically clear between £50–£150/MWh (5–15p/kWh). Network & policy levies include DUoS, BSUoS (~1.4p/kWh), and environmental levies (~3.5–5.0p/kWh).

Empirical Behavioural Elasticity & the Zero-Price Effect

Why "£0.00" beats a 50% discount

A 2026 NBER study (Lopez Garcia, Metcalfe, Schein & Sun, with the Centre for Net Zero and Octopus Energy) ran natural field trials across 60,000 households in Great Britain and Spain, measuring demand response across four incentive levels.

50% Price Discount (PeakSave model)
+14.3%
Free Electricity (£0.00/kWh)
+31.0%
Free Electricity + 5p/kWh Payment
+33.8%
Free Electricity + 15p/kWh Payment
+34.0%

Non-linear zero-price effect: going from a 50% discount to fully free electricity more than doubles the response (+14.3% → +31.0%). Zero acts as a psychological catalyst that a linear discount doesn't.

Diminishing returns beyond free: paying customers on top of free power adds very little — an extra 15p/kWh only lifts response from +31.0% to +34.0%. £0.00/kWh alone captures over 90% of the achievable demand-side flexibility, without the added cost.

Research Gaps, Data Limitations & Resolution Framework

What this report can't verify — on purpose

Public market codes, trial publications, and supplier announcements establish the technical mechanics. Proprietary boundaries remain in how suppliers actually operate — stated plainly rather than filled in with guesses.

Proprietary dispatch solvers & model weights

Kraken Asset, Kaluza, and Centrica Hive don't publish the objective function weights, solver configurations, or LP heuristics used to balance assets across wholesale, BM, and ancillary markets in real time.

Confidential bilateral DNO tender clearing rates

Exact contract pricing, reservation fees, and clearing rates negotiated between suppliers and DNOs (e.g. UK Power Networks, SSEN) under private flexibility frameworks remain commercially sensitive.

Internal corporate ledger cost allocations

Retailers don't disclose the exact accounting split allocating net flexibility costs between wholesale trading desks, balancing cash-out reserves, and marketing/CAC budgets.

Resolution pathways
1
Empirical proxy simulation

Build an open-source simulation (Pyomo/HiGHS) combining public EPEX pricing, Elexon BSC System Prices, and open smart-meter datasets (Centre for Net Zero, Low Carbon London) to model a 100,000-meter supplier's margins during constraint events.

2
Regulatory ledger reconstruction

Reconstruct financial allocations from statutory filings — Centrica, Octopus, and OVO annual reports alongside Ofgem's Consolidated Segmental Statements — to benchmark supply margins against CAC and churn.

3
Licence condition extraction

Examine DNO flexibility procurement statements filed with Ofgem under SLC31E, alongside NESO's Local Constraint Market rulebooks, to establish empirical compensation ranges for domestic turn-up.
